— Frequently asked

FAQ

+ How much can I earn as a contributor?

Depends on your hardware (GFLOPS), uptime, and how much capacity you offer. A typical laptop running 50% offered, 8h/day can earn $5–30/month. A gaming PC at 24/7 can hit $50–150/month. Earnings scale linearly with active time + compute power.

+ Is my data safe? Does HIMER see anything?

No. The browser agent runs in a fully isolated WebAssembly sandbox. It cannot read your files, browser history, cookies, passwords, webcam, or microphone. It only processes anonymized math packages sent by paying clients. Source code is open on GitHub for audit.

+ When and how do I get paid?

Earnings accumulate in real-time. Withdraw available at $500+ balance. Two payout methods: USDC crypto on Polygon (instant, $0.01 fee) or Bank wire transfer (1–3 days, EU/US). KYC verification on first withdrawal. Admin approval ~24h.

+ Is HIMER legal in my country?

HIMER is fully legal worldwide — same legal model as Folding@home, BOINC, and SETI@home (all running for 20+ years). You voluntarily share unused compute. Payouts may be taxable income in your country — consult a local tax advisor.

+ What can I use HIMER for as a developer?

AI/ML training & inference, 3D rendering (Blender), scientific simulations, distributed web scraping, batch data processing, image/video conversions. Anything that can be split into parallel chunks.

+ How does HIMER make money?

Business clients pay for compute via API. HIMER takes 60% as platform fee (covers infrastructure, support, R&D), 40% goes to contributors. We also offer subscription plans (Starter $29, Pro $149, Enterprise custom) and pay-as-you-go at $0.0002/GFLOP-s.

— Technical Documentation

How HIMER works from the inside.

From a click on "Join" to earning your first dollar — every step is transparent, cryptographic, and auditable.

Step 1 · Connect

You click "Join". The browser generates a stable device fingerprint (no cookies, no PII) and registers a new node. Hardware capacity is detected: CPU cores, RAM, network bandwidth. You get a User ID like U-XXXXXXXX. Save it — that's how you sign in from other devices.

Step 2 · Sandbox launch

A WebAssembly worker is spawned in your browser tab. It's isolated — cannot access files, cookies, passwords, webcam, or microphone. It only talks to HIMER orchestrator and only receives signed compute packages.

Step 3 · Job dispatch

A paying client posts a compute job (e.g., "matrix multiply 10000×10000"). Our orchestrator splits it into chunks, picks the best nodes based on capacity/trust/region, and dispatches. Your node receives a chunk, processes it.

Step 4 · Proof-of-Compute verification

Your node returns the result + a cryptographic hash. The orchestrator picks 5% of results at random and re-runs them on independent "witness" nodes. If results match → trust score increases, you get paid. If they don't match → trust drops, no pay for that chunk.

Step 5 · Payment

Earnings accumulate in your wallet in real-time. At $500, you can request withdrawal via USDC (Polygon) or bank wire. Admin approves within ~24h. Funds arrive instantly (crypto) or 1–3 business days (bank).

1. Proof-of-Compute

Each node reports heartbeat every 5 seconds. For each task, the node returns result + a SHA-256 cryptographic hash. 5% of results are re-verified on witness nodes. If hashes match, trust score increases; if they diverge, trust drops and earnings for that chunk are forfeited. Below 50 trust → node is shadow-banned from high-value jobs.

2. Earnings Formula

Transparent calculation per second of active contribution. Three resources are billed: CPU compute (GFLOPS), RAM offered (GB-hours), bandwidth used (GB transferred).

CPU value= GFLOPS × load% × $0.0001
RAM value= GB-hours × $0.00003
BW value= MB/s × seconds × $0.00002
Gross per second= sum × boost_multiplier
Your share (40%)= Gross × 0.4
HIMER platform fee (60%)= Gross × 0.6

Premium boost: contributors active 100+ hours get 1.3× boost automatically. Referral bonus: every active contributor you refer gives you 5% of their earnings for 12 months (paid by HIMER, not from their wallet).

3. Withdraw

Minimum withdraw $500. Fee 2% covers payment processing. Two methods: USDC crypto on Polygon (instant, $0.01 network fee) or bank wire transfer (1–3 business days, requires IBAN + ID verification). Withdrawal available after platform has received corresponding client payments — typical lag 1–2 weeks for new accounts, instant for established ones.

4. Multi-device

One User ID can be used on unlimited devices (phone, laptop, tablet, family PCs). Each device becomes a separate node and contributes to the same wallet. Useful for: family households (everyone running it), small businesses (office computers idle at night), or single users with multiple machines.

5. Architecture & infrastructure

Backend: Node.js orchestrator (open-source), Supabase Postgres for state, WebSocket for real-time. Frontend: vanilla HTML/JS PWA, no framework lock-in, installable on any device. Edge: contributing nodes run signed WASM workloads in browser sandbox. Payments: Stripe Connect (bank) + Polygon network (USDC). Open-source: github.com/himer-network (everything except payment keys is public and auditable).

— Privacy Policy

Privacy by Design.

Your privacy isn't a setting you have to turn on — it's built into the architecture. Here's exactly how, in plain language.

Isolation Architecture

  • → WebAssembly Sandbox: The compute agent runs inside the browser's WASM sandbox — the same security model that lets you safely run untrusted code from any website. It physically cannot read files, access other tabs, or escape the sandbox.
  • → Network whitelist: The agent only communicates with HIMER's orchestrator. It cannot phone home to third parties or exfiltrate anything.
  • → Signed workloads only: Every compute package is cryptographically signed. Unsigned or tampered code is rejected before execution.
  • → Open-source & auditable: The entire agent is public on GitHub. Anyone — security researchers, you, your IT team — can read every line.

What HIMER can NEVER access

  • ✗ Your personal files, documents, photos
  • ✗ Passwords, cookies, login sessions
  • ✗ Browsing history or other tabs
  • ✗ Webcam, microphone, location
  • ✗ Keystrokes or screen contents
  • ✗ Banking, email, or any app data

What HIMER actually does

  • ✓ Processes anonymized math packages
  • ✓ Reports only CPU/RAM/bandwidth metrics
  • ✓ Uses an anonymous HIMER ID (no name/email needed)
  • ✓ Runs only when you allow it
  • ✓ Stops instantly when you close the tab
  • ✓ Lets you set how much you contribute

Data we collect

Minimal, by design. To operate the network we store: an anonymous device fingerprint (to recognize returning devices), aggregate capacity metrics (CPU cores, RAM, bandwidth — never their contents), node performance stats (uptime, GFLOPS, trust score), and earnings/payout records. If you provide an email for payouts, it's used only for notifications and withdrawals — never sold, never shared.

For business clients

Your workloads are processed across anonymous nodes. Inputs and outputs are encrypted in transit. Results carry a cryptographic hash so you can verify integrity. We don't inspect, store, or reuse your job data beyond what's needed to deliver results and bill accurately. EU clients can request region-pinned processing for GDPR compliance.

Your rights (GDPR & global)

You can request a full export of your data, or complete deletion, at any time. Email himer.nodes@gmail.com — all data is removed within 30 days. There's no lock-in: stop contributing whenever you want, your accumulated earnings remain payable.

Comparison to similar projects

HIMER follows the same proven, legal model as Folding@home, BOINC, and SETI@home — distributed volunteer computing that's run safely for over 20 years. The key difference: we add transparent compensation and a real client economy, while keeping the same strict privacy guarantees.

— About

Why HIMER?

The compute economy is broken. We're fixing it — one device at a time.

The problem

Artificial intelligence and intensive compute now drive the global economy. Training a single large model can cost millions in cloud bills. Yet the power to run these workloads is concentrated in the hands of just a few hyperscalers — AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure. They set the prices, control the access, and capture nearly all the value. For a startup, a researcher, or a creator, compute is expensive, gated, and centralized.

Meanwhile, billions of personal computers, laptops, and phones sit idle most of the day. The collective unused compute power of consumer devices worldwide dwarfs every datacenter combined. That power is simply wasted.

Our solution

HIMER is a decentralized compute network (DePIN) that turns idle devices into a global supercomputer. Contributors share their unused CPU, RAM, and bandwidth. Businesses and creators rent that capacity through a simple API — at a fraction of cloud prices. Everyone wins: contributors earn passive income, clients save up to 90%, and compute becomes democratized.

We didn't invent volunteer computing — projects like Folding@home, BOINC, and SETI@home proved decades ago that distributed compute works at planetary scale. What HIMER adds is a real economy: cryptographic proof-of-compute for trust, transparent payouts for contributors, and a production-grade API for paying customers.

Our mission

Make anyone with a computer a cloud provider — and make compute affordable for everyone.

Our principles

  • ◆ Open-source first. Every line of the agent is auditable. No black boxes.
  • ◆ Privacy by design. The sandbox can never touch your files, passwords, or camera.
  • ◆ Fair rewards. Contributors are paid transparently, with a formula published on every page.
  • ◆ Sustainable. Reusing idle hardware has a near-zero carbon footprint vs. building new datacenters.

Where we're headed

Today, HIMER runs in the browser — instant, zero-install, accessible to anyone. Next comes the desktop agent (GPU support for AI training and 3D rendering), then enterprise SLAs, a P2P marketplace, and full Web3 settlement. We're building the infrastructure layer for the next decade of compute.
